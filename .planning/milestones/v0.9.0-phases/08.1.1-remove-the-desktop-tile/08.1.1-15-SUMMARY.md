## Decisions Made

### Deleted tests (Chesterton: why each existed, and why it can go)

- **FourthTests `aHiddenDesktopKeepsItsWeightAndTheOthersScaleIntoTheRest`:** this pinned that a hidden Desktop kept its stored weight while W took ¼ of the visible part. Under D-01 and D-09 nothing can hide a Desktop any more. The general rule, that a child not taking part keeps its weight, is still pinned by `aChildNotPresentKeepsItsWeightAndTakesNoPartInThePosition` and by the absent rows of `theSpanIsWhatTheWindowGets`.
- **FourthTests `aDockedSingleRootLeafBehavesLikeTheDesktopLeaf`:** this compared a docked leaf root with the old Desktop leaf. The `[A 1]` table rows now pin the same two results (1st and middle) on both axes, so the test added nothing.
- **TabGroupModelTests `theLastDockedLeafLeavingShowsAHiddenDesktop`:** its only subject was that removing the last leaf un-hides the Desktop (D-24 and D-31, retired by D-01 and D-05).

### Kept subjects, changed setups

- `aChildNotPresent…`: the third child is the docked tile C (weight ¼) instead of D. If D were simply dropped, A would be the only tile taking part and the test would no longer exercise "absent B takes no part in the position". The arithmetic is unchanged: [A 3/16, B ¼, W 3/16, C ⅜].
- `withNothingElseTakingPart…` (both tests): `[A ½, B ½]` with both absent, instead of A absent and D hidden. The "lone hidden Desktop" case became a lone absent leaf root A: [W ½, A ½].
- `theSpanIsWhatTheWindowGets`: D became B (or A on a lone root), with the same weights and the same spans. A hidden D became an absent window, so the arithmetic is the same.
- `applyDropInsertsTheFourth` uses `twoDocked`: [A ¼, W ¼, B ½]. `aTabGroupIsOneChild…`: [A ½, tabs ½], and on a lone tab-group root W goes to the 1st column as [W ¼, tabs ¾].
- PersistenceTests `olderFileDecodesThroughTheChain`: the `isDesktopHidden == nil` check went. The rich world has no Desktop tile left to drop, and DesktopMigrationTests is the suite that covers dropping a legacy one.
