## Phase Goal

**As a** user, **I want to** have docked windows fill the whole screen and floating windows sit on top of them like normal macOS windows, **so that** I never have to learn or manage a "Desktop tile".

<objective>
Make every half, third and fourth exact (8.1.1 D-16, rules E1-E4 from 08.1.1-20-PLAN.md). `insertFourth` stops growing a window into an empty side. It uses empty space first, shrinks docked windows only when that runs out, and leaves the rest empty as `Node.spacer` areas. A docked window moved to a position leaves its old place empty. Labels name the position, since the window always gets exactly it.

Purpose: this is the user's UAT issue 1 ("docking one to the Left half made it fill the whole screen").
Output: the new arithmetic in `Fourths.swift`, position-only `FourthCopy` labels, the vacate-on-move rule in `Arranger.dropping`, and re-derived FourthTests and ArrangerFourthTests.
</objective>

Implements 8.1.1 D-16 rules E2 and E3 in full. Write the rows first (the table type already runs both axes).

The arithmetic, along `axis`, over the root's children taking part (spacers always take part), with lengths normalised to the room they share:
1. The window's span is [a, b]. Cut every empty area by [a, b]: the part before a stays on the before side, the part after b on the after side, and the part inside is used up.
2. A tile goes after when a == 0, before when b == 1, and otherwise before only when its centre is before the window's centre (a tile centred exactly at the window's centre goes after, as today). The order stays as it was.
3. Fit each side to its room (a before, 1 - b after). Too long: shrink that side's empty pieces first, proportionally. If they run out, drop them and scale the side's tiles to fit, as today. Too short: add an empty piece next to the window for the rest (E3). No pieces at all: one empty piece of the room.
4. New children: before pieces, the window, after pieces. Children not taking part keep their stored weight and stay after the piece that preceded them. A piece shorter than `LayoutTree.minWeight` is dropped. Then normalise (spacers merge) and validate. All or nothing.

A root that is a leaf, or splits across `axis`, is one child spanning [0, 1]. First take its empty margins along `axis`. A spacer's margin is all of it, and a tile has none. A split along `axis` has its leading spacer children plus the leading margin of the first non-empty child. A split across `axis` has the smallest leading margin among its children. Trailing margins work the same way. Treat those margins as empty pieces beside the trimmed block. Trimming removes the same length from every child of a cross split, and from the leading or trailing spacers of a split along `axis`. Implement this as a function over any node, not only the root, so 08.1.1-23's corners can call it on a child.

Keep it iterative where it walks the tree (trees are at most `maxDepth` deep, but stay consistent with the file). Document the rules E2 and E3 in the function's doc, briefly.
